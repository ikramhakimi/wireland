# RR HairClinic — Voice & Tone

## Purpose

Help people understand their hair concerns, explore treatment options, and feel comfortable taking the next step.
Support informed decisions and set clear expectations about what an assessment involves.

Apply these guidelines when writing or editing user-facing copy. Keep changes within the requested scope.

## Brand Voice

Understanding. Informed. Clear.

Write in clear, natural English. Take hair concerns seriously and explain options in everyday language.
Sound like a knowledgeable professional who listens carefully and gives practical guidance.

## Empathetic and Respectful

- Acknowledge concerns without assuming how someone feels.
- Welcome people who are noticing changes, comparing treatments, or simply exploring.
- Avoid shame, fear, and language that treats hair loss as a personal failure.
- Never imply that someone needs more hair to be attractive, confident, or complete.
- Avoid dismissing a concern by describing all hair loss as natural or harmless.

Avoid: “Don't wait until you're completely bald.”

Prefer: “Noticing changes in your hair? We can help you understand your options.”

## Scientifically Grounded, Easy to Understand

- Explain what a treatment does, who it may suit, and relevant considerations using confirmed information.
- Introduce medical terms only when useful, and explain them plainly.
- Keep medical claims specific and supported. Do not invent evidence or imply guaranteed results.
- Describe doctor involvement and personalisation accurately.
- Acknowledge that treatment suitability and results vary where relevant to a decision.

Avoid: “A proven formula that guarantees thicker hair.”

Prefer: “Explore treatments for hair loss and learn which options may suit your needs.”

## Guiding and Transparent

- Give people a clear next step and enough information to decide whether to take it.
- For assessments, explain what is involved, what information is requested, and what happens afterwards.
- Mention timing, cost, privacy, or commitment only when confirmed.
- Avoid promising a diagnosis or treatment recommendation unless the service actually provides it.
- Use “assessment” and “consultation” according to the actual service; do not treat them as interchangeable.

Avoid: “Your perfect treatment is just one question away.”

Prefer, if it matches the actual process:
“Answer a few questions about your hair concerns to help us understand your needs.”

## Calm and Direct

- Use short, natural sentences and concrete language.
- Keep the tone warm, professional, and approachable.
- Avoid being overly familiar, playful, or sentimental.
- Avoid hype, forced urgency, and excessive exclamation marks.
- Avoid vague promises such as “transform your confidence” or “unlock your best self.”

Avoid: “Your ultimate hair transformation starts NOW!”

Prefer: “Understand your hair. Explore your options.”

## Interface Label Capitalization

- Use Title Case for button labels (including links styled as buttons) and form field labels.
- Capitalize the first and last words, plus nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs.
  Keep short articles, coordinating conjunctions, and prepositions lowercase unless first or last.
- Preserve established brand names and acronyms, such as RR HairClinic, Google, Apple, and SMS.
- Write capitalization directly in the source text, component data, or translation string.
  Do not use CSS `text-transform: capitalize`, Tailwind `capitalize`, or runtime case conversion.
- Keep descriptions, placeholders, helper text, validation errors, and full sentences in sentence case.
- Apply this convention when creating or editing labels within the requested scope; do not rewrite unrelated copy.

| Context | Preferred | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Button | Create Account | Create account |
| Button | Log In | Log in |
| Button | Continue with Google | Continue With Google |
| Field label | Email Address | Email address |
| Field label | Full Name | Full name |
| Placeholder | Enter your email address | Enter Your Email Address |

## Calls to Action

Use labels that describe the next action accurately. Match the label to the destination or behaviour.

| User intent | Suggested CTA |
| --- | --- |
| Discover products | Explore Treatments |
| Understand the process | How It Works |
| Begin the assessment | Start Assessment |
| Learn about a product | View Treatment |
| Contact the team | Ask a Question |

## Copy Review Checklist

- Does the copy respect the reader without assuming insecurity?
- Is the meaning clear on the first read?
- Are medical and service claims supported?
- Does the CTA accurately describe the next step?
- Do button and field labels use Title Case in the source text, without CSS or runtime capitalization?
- Are limitations explained where they affect a decision?
- Have pressure, shame, and promises of guaranteed results been removed?

## Voice Statement

RR HairClinic helps people understand their hair concerns through clear information and considerate guidance,
so they can make informed decisions about their next step.
